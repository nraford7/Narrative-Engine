## Drift Analysis

### Semantic match — One Thing
Partial. The opening states the selected stability focal, but the cold-read takeaway is the allocation decision and its price-pressure tradeoff. “Retention metrics held steady in Q3” is background to the strongest landing beat rather than the single point retained.

### Ask delivery
Yes. The final section explicitly says “Choose option (c) at the offsite: reallocate AED 1.8M to guided onboarding” and repeats “Choose the reallocation.” Delivery of the Ask is clear even though its rationale is not developed.

### Through-Line carriage
Yes. The piece follows the prescribed sequence of metrics, operating continuity, and the offsite decision. The sequence is visible, but the transitions do not establish how the status report supports the budget choice.

### Engagement check
The opening raises what explains the retention decline and what executives should do about it. Attention drops in the CS operational update, then recovers at the explicit decision. The ending answers only the action portion: it leaves unresolved why onboarding is the response to an opening dominated by price pressure. This incomplete loop blocks PASS independently of the partial semantic match.

### Shape gravity check
An answer-first memo naturally places its main payload in the opening, then supports and reinforces it. Here, the opening names stability, while the final imperative and its sales tradeoff deliver a stronger, insufficiently connected payload. This is fixable within the existing three-section memo by aligning the opening question, the status evidence, and the closing synthesis; the shape does not inherently demand a different kind of conclusion.

### Source Check outcome (Phase A2)
A2 found the omitted onboarding findings more consequential for the executive team's allocation decision than the piece's unsupported recommendation:

> Second, the strongest single predictor of renewal in the entire dataset was days-to-first-saved-report during onboarding: accounts that built and saved a report within 14 days renewed at 96%, versus 71% for accounts that took longer than 45 days, regardless of price paid, segment, or discount status. Exit-interview price citations concentrate almost entirely in the slow-onboarding cohort.

These findings explain the 14-day milestone and challenge treating price objections as sufficient grounds for more discounts. The source's preliminary modeling further connects this evidence to the allocation tradeoff. The brief explicitly excludes this analysis, so the omission is a focal-selection issue as well as a gap in the decision rationale. Because the focal is user-selected-after-reset, this finding is ADVISORY only; it does not trigger a focal reset or determine the verdict above.

## Recommended Action

### If NEEDS_REVISION
- **What to change:** Frame the opening around the narrow question the selected focal can answer: whether Q3 signals a broad departure from segment retention ranges. Keep the 1.1-percentage-point GRR decline explicit. Distinguish operational updates from evidence of retention stability, and connect them as operating context. Close by resolving that status question using the reported segment ranges, then state option (c) as the requested budget decision. Make clear that the descriptive metrics alone do not establish onboarding's superiority; do not imply a causal rationale absent from this selected argument.
- **What to keep:** The explicit option (c), AED 1.8M amount, 14-day milestone, mid-market and SMB scope, sales tradeoff, segment figures, and unsoftened GRR decline. Preserve the user's narrow focal and the existing three-section answer-first structure.
- **Why this is fixable in revision:** The selected focal is already present at the opening and the Ask is explicit. A careful editor can strengthen the stability takeaway and close a properly scoped opening question through framing and closing edits without replacing the shape or importing the excluded analysis.
