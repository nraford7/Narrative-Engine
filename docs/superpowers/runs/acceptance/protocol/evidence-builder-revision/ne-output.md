# Q4 retention allocation

## Reallocate AED 1.8M of the retention-discount pool to guided onboarding.

The cohort evidence and preliminary modeling favor this shift. Sales has requested an expanded Q4 discount pool, currently AED 2.6M per year, while gross revenue retention has fallen 1.1 percentage points quarter on quarter to 91.2%. Price pressure is real: three of the seven largest churned accounts cited cost in exit interviews. The cohort results give us reason to scrutinize further discount spending.

## The cohort links renewal more strongly to onboarding speed.

The analysis covers 1,240 accounts renewing in the last 24 months. Accounts that received a retention discount churned at 2.3 times the rate of undiscounted accounts within 18 months of the concession. This is an observational association; it does not establish that discounts caused churn.

Days to first saved report during onboarding was the strongest single predictor of renewal in the dataset. Accounts that built and saved a report within 14 days renewed at 96%, versus 71% for those taking longer than 45 days, regardless of price paid, segment, or discount status. Exit-interview price citations concentrated almost entirely in the slow-onboarding cohort. These results support the proposed onboarding focus, while leaving its causal effect on retention unproven.

## Choose option (c) at the offsite, accepting tougher Q4 price conversations.

Option (c) funds a guided-onboarding service for all new mid-market and SMB accounts, targeting a first saved report within 14 days. Preliminary modeling suggests it affects 18-month retention 3-4 times more per dirham than discounting. That estimate remains preliminary. Approve the AED 1.8M reallocation with the explicit tradeoff that sales must absorb tougher price conversations in Q4.
