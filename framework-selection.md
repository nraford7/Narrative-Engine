# Framework Selection Reference

Choose structures by the material and the intended reader outcome, not by content category. This reference is used by the orchestrator and reviewers; the writer receives only the selected, compiled instructions.

## Inputs and timing

Use the assignment (purpose, success, audience starting position, format/use/limits), full Material Read, and provisional points from Phases 1.5–2. No final focal is required yet. Compare structures in Phase 3; approve point and structure together in Phase 3.5. On revision, preserve a user-owned point unless the user changes it.

## Selection protocol

### 1. Establish direct explanation

Sketch the plain reasoning first: findings, explanation, implications, intended outcome. Answer-first is a useful baseline, not an automatic winner. If the inference chain fails, repair it before considering dramatic order.

### 2. Find supported alternatives

Match the kind of takeaway to a small candidate set:

| Takeaway | Candidate arcs | What their payoff does |
|---|---|---|
| Counterintuitive reveal or reframe | Prestige, Mystery Box, Trojan Horse | Reinterprets the preceding information. |
| Strategic decision or execution path | Time Machine, Heist | Makes a choice or route to the goal intelligible. |
| Causal explanation | Columbo | Explains how an already-stated outcome happened. |
| Transformation | Hero's Journey, Freytag | Shows a supported change through events and consequences. |
| Synthesis across views | Rashomon | Explains the relationship between supported competing accounts. |
| Hidden pattern | Game of the Scene | Makes a recurring pattern recognizable. |

These are candidates, not automatic assignments. Consider 2–4 at most; if there is no useful match, direct explanation is sufficient. Content-type, audience and tone categories cannot supply missing events, opposition or evidence.

**Reveal gate:** Prestige, Mystery Box, Trojan Horse and any unnamed withheld-reveal treatment require a genuine surprise supported by a source passage and the audience starting position. An uncertain audience hypothesis is not proof of surprise. If the Material Read says none, exclude these choices. Other arcs need no manufactured surprise: Columbo gives the outcome first; Heist can explain execution; Rashomon can synthesize openly.

### 3. Audit source support

Use the beat weights in `narrative-arcs.md`. Every **anchor/essential** beat requires quoted source support. Missing essential beat → reject the arc, not an assignment to invent or remove the beat. Nonessential standard/brief/optional beats may be omitted or combined when the source or budget warrants it. Preserve any qualification necessary to keep a claim true regardless of beat weight.

For each candidate record: beat → actual source passage → what it establishes. A section title or paraphrase without a source anchor is insufficient. For scenarios quote the assumptions/conditions, retaining their hypothetical status. An illustrative event cannot be presented as an observed fact.

### 4. Test the proposed progression (skeleton stamp)

Write the opening, progression, payoff and close against the provisional point. Pass only if:
- Payoff delivers the intended claim/insight or bounded answer.
- Close fulfills the purpose and success condition (the Ask can be understanding, not action).
- The reasoning remains intact under this order, with necessary context supplied before it is needed.
- The essential beats and required evidence fit the user’s limits.

A transformation story that ends in personal change does not automatically land an operational decision. Reject a mismatch rather than adding an unrelated final recommendation. A second adjacent candidate set may be considered once; never relax source requirements to rescue an arc.

### 5. Present a meaningful comparison

Offer direct explanation and the best eligible arc, plus a second arc only when it changes the experience usefully. If no arc survives, explain the relevant rejection and present direct explanation without apology.

For each option show:
- **Point:** provisional main claim/insight.
- **Opening:** actual source-supported entry point.
- **Reader's question:** what keeps this audience reading; relevance can suffice without suspense.
- **Progression:** short outline of how understanding develops.
- **Payoff:** what becomes clear and how it fulfills the purpose.
- **Source support:** passages for essential beats, with qualifications.
- **Trade-off:** speed, explanatory burden, length or delayed understanding.

Do not show a ceremonial ranking of ten arcs. In Deep the user compares concrete options; in Fast mark the recommended combination in the approval brief. Neither mode silently locks a focal before this comparison.

## Communication frameworks and emotional pacing

After the comparison, a framework from `communication-frameworks.md` can clarify delivery: Pyramid groups reasons; SCQA frames a question; other approaches may help a supported persuasive case. Compile only the useful operation into the outline. Do not stack two incompatible sequences or impose numerical allocations from example decks.

Emotional guidance in `emotional-arcs.md` describes possible effects of supported events. It never requires agitation, suspense, false trails, triumph, quotes or a mid-piece turn. Tone and audience preferences can lower intensity; neither creates facts.

## Final handoff and review

Approve the point and structure together. The brief contains the complete retained skeleton, source anchors, omitted nonessential beats, rationale and practical pacing. The writer does not open this catalog.

The blind judge reads the output before the brief, then assesses whether the structure delivers the point and purpose. A structure that pulls toward another payoff receives FRAMEWORK_MISMATCH. These are model judgments, not empirical proof that one arc is more engaging. Compare actual outputs before claiming an improvement.
