# Audience Profiles — Starting Suggestions

> **Reference layer.** Read by the orchestrator (brief compilation, shape selection) and by judges/reviewers. Never read by the build subagent — a rule that matters is compiled into the Build Brief.

Use the audience category as an initial hypothesis. Specific knowledge, beliefs, concerns and objections supplied by the user take precedence. Do not claim a group always thinks or feels a particular way. Unknowns remain unknown; inferred preferences are labelled. Purpose, evidence, the approved argument and practical limits govern structure and the Ask.

Compile only relevant guidance as concrete instructions. Every selected instruction should explain what it helps this audience understand or decide. No mandatory financial metric, persuasion device, emotional state, recommendation or numeric headline applies across all assignments.

| Audience | Useful questions | Possible evidence and expression |
|---|---|---|
| Executive / Board | Which decision or understanding is needed? What constraints and risks matter? | Clear implications, relevant operational/financial evidence when available, explicit uncertainty and alternatives. Answer-first often helps; an educational brief need not request approval. |
| Technical / Engineering | What mechanism, assumption or trade-off is disputed? How much do they already know? | Methods, reproducibility, boundary conditions and comparisons. State the result early when useful, then explain the mechanism; no mandatory inductive order. |
| Sales / Marketing | Which customer problem or commercial question matters here? | Actual customer examples, relevant outcomes and objections. Do not invent urgency, testimonials or competitive threats. |
| Investors / VCs | Which opportunity, uncertainty or investment decision is being assessed? | Source-supported economics, traction, assumptions and downside. Preserve estimates and distinguish scenarios from forecasts and actual results. |
| General Public | What context is missing? What makes this relevant? | Common language and concrete source examples. Define specialist terms without assuming low intelligence or a need for entertainment. |
| Skeptics / Resisters | What do they dispute, and why? What would count as evidence? | Address the actual objection, alternatives and limits. Do not caricature resistance or presume that persuasion is the purpose. |
| Mixed / Cross-functional | What is shared knowledge? Where do meanings or incentives differ? | Shared definitions, connections between perspectives and enough context to make the reasoning accessible across roles. |
| Academic / Research | What question, evidence standard or existing explanation is at issue? | Methods, provenance, limitations and contribution. The approved purpose decides whether the piece explains, advocates or questions. |

## Brief compilation

Record:
1. Known starting position, with source (user or material).
2. Unknowns and explicitly inferred hypotheses.
3. The understanding/action gap the piece addresses.
4. Evidence that addresses that gap, and limits that may prevent it.
5. Relevant trust signals, resistance concerns, terminology and presentation needs.

A reader should be able to trace a recommendation back to the actual assignment: "They dispute the sample's relevance, so explain its selection and limits before generalizing." "Technical audience → add jargon" is not an adequate instruction.
