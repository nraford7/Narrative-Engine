# Presentation narrative: refusing an unsupported transformation

Fictional example. The filename is retained for existing links; this is standalone Narrative Engine presentation writing.

## Source and assignment

A facilities team piloted a new heating schedule in one building for two weeks. Recorded energy use was 8% lower than in the preceding two weeks. Outdoor temperatures were milder. No weather-adjusted estimate, cost analysis or controlled comparison was available.

The user explicitly chooses Deep, an educational five-slide presentation for non-specialist staff, and supplies the point: “This pilot is promising, but we cannot yet attribute the reduction to the schedule.” Staff are interested in savings; their views on causation are unknown. Success means they can distinguish an observed change from an established cause. Sentence-led presentation, concise detail, non-specialist knowledge, conversational rhythm and balanced tone.

## Material Read

The observed comparison supports an 8% reduction over those periods, not an 8% intervention effect or a recurring saving. Milder weather is a contrary explanation. This distinction directly serves the educational purpose. No verified surprise is available.

## Candidate and arc decision

The supplied point remains `user-stated`; there is no need to invent alternative points. If the user requests Hero's Journey, explain that the source supplies no supported ordeal or transformation. Reject the unsupported arc and propose direct explanation. Do not turn a tentative pilot into a victory narrative.

## Approved written sequence

1. This pilot shows a reduction, not yet its cause.
2. Energy use fell 8% between the two periods.
3. Milder weather could also explain the change.
4. A fair comparison must account for that difference.
5. The schedule's effect therefore remains uncertain.

Each slide's supporting text keeps the one-building, two-week limits visible where relevant. A proposed next analysis is identified as a proposal, not something already performed. The brief and review inspect comprehension, not excitement or a funding request. The deliverable is the written slide narrative; no visual rendering is required.
